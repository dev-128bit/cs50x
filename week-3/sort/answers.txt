sort1 uses: Bubble Sort

How do you know?:
    - The 'sort1' sorts the random5000.txt values under 0.060259 seconds, as I used python's 'time' module to compute the start and end times.
    - By using that, I confirmed the 'sort1' has significantly higher compute time when compared to others.
    - The higher compute time expresses that there are significant amount of comparisons made that slowed the system.
    - With comparision among sorting techniques (Merge, Bubble, Selection Sorts), the bubble sort is the slowest and requires large compute time.
    - So I decided that 'sort1' uses the Bubble Sort technique.

sort2 uses: Merge Sort

How do you know?:
    - The 'sort2' sorts the random5000.txt values under 0.031762 seconds, as I used python's 'time' module to compute.
    - Compared to the 'sort1' and 'sort3', the 'sort2' compute time is very low.
    - As the compute time describes the low value when compared to others, I decided it would be merge sort.
    - The Merge Sort uses the divide and conquer rule to sort the un-sorted values, which significantly increases the time efficiency.
    - As the Merge sort has O(n log n) time complexity, this explains why 'sort2' is significantly fast. Hence, it is Merge Sort.

sort3 uses: Selection Sort

How do you know?:
    - The 'sort3' sorts the random5000.txt values under 0.043922 seconds, again with python's 'time' module to compute.
    - Compared to the 'sort1', the 'sort3' performs a bit faster. When compared to 'sort2', the 'sort3' performs a bit slower.
    - By this 'sort3' stands between 'sort2' and 'sort1'.
    - As the both 'sort2' and 'sort1' are already decided and the remaining sorting technique is Selection Sort.
    - The Selection Sort is slower than Merge Sort and it is faster than Bubble Sort.
    - The Selection Sort involves less swapping operations when compared to bubble sort. As this explains the Selection Sort performs better than Bubble Sort.
    - Thus proving the 'sort3' uses Selection Sort.
